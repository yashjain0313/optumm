===================================================
  US HEALTHCARE — SIMPLE EXPLANATION (No Jargon)
===================================================

First understand this: In the US, healthcare is NOT free.
Every doctor visit, medicine, surgery — you pay for it.
That's why health "insurance" exists and why companies
like Optum / UnitedHealth are massive businesses.

---

THE BASIC STORY (How US healthcare works)
------------------------------------------

Imagine you're working at a US company (say, HCL or Infosys USA).
Your company gives you a "health plan" — basically a deal where:

  YOU pay a little bit every month  →  called a PREMIUM
  The insurance covers big medical bills when needed

But even with insurance, you still pay SOME amount when you
visit a doctor. That's where these 3 things come in:

  1. DEDUCTIBLE
  2. COPAY
  3. OUT-OF-POCKET MAX

Let's understand each with a story.


---

1. DEDUCTIBLE — "Pay first, then insurance helps"
-------------------------------------------------
Imagine your plan says: "Deductible = $1,700"

This means: At the start of every year (Jan 1),
you have to pay the FIRST $1,700 of your medical bills YOURSELF.
Insurance won't pay anything until you've spent $1,700.

Example:
  Jan → Hospital bill = $500   → YOU pay $500  (total paid: $500)
  Feb → Hospital bill = $800   → YOU pay $800  (total paid: $1,300)
  Mar → Doctor bill   = $400   → YOU pay $400  (total paid: $1,700)
                                 ← DEDUCTIBLE MET! Insurance kicks in now.
  Apr → Hospital bill = $600   → Insurance pays most of it.

So in our app, "deductible_met" = how much you've already paid this year.
"deductible_remaining" = how much more before insurance starts helping.


---

2. COPAY — "Fixed small amount per visit"
------------------------------------------
After your deductible is met, you don't pay the full bill.
You just pay a fixed small amount called a COPAY.

Example:
  Primary care doctor visit → you pay $25 (copay), insurance pays rest
  Specialist (cardiologist)  → you pay $50 (copay), insurance pays rest
  Urgent Care               → you pay $75 (copay), insurance pays rest
  Generic medicine           → you pay $10 (copay), insurance pays rest

Think of copay like a "entry fee" for medical services.
It's fixed. It's predictable. You always know what you'll pay.


---

3. OUT-OF-POCKET MAX — "Your worst-case limit"
-----------------------------------------------
This is a safety net.

If in one year you had terrible health luck and paid $8,500
in total medical bills... insurance pays 100% for rest of the year.

"Out-of-pocket max" = the most you'll EVER pay in one year.
After hitting this, everything is FREE for that year.

Self-only plans: max $8,500/year
Family plans:    max $17,000/year


---

NOW: HSA — Health Savings Account
-----------------------------------
Problem: medical bills before deductible is met are expensive.
Solution: HSA — a special bank account just for medical expenses.

Rules:
  - Tax-free money (government doesn't tax it — huge benefit)
  - You + your employer both put money in
  - You use it to pay doctor bills, medicines, etc.
  - Unused money CARRIES OVER to next year (it's yours forever)
  - IRS (government) sets how much you can put in per year:
      Self-only: max $4,400/year (2026)
      Family:    max $8,750/year (2026)

Think of HSA like a medical piggy bank that the government
doesn't tax — and your company also adds money to it.

In our app: hsa_balance = how much money is sitting in their HSA.


---

FSA — Flexible Spending Account
---------------------------------
Similar to HSA but different rules:

  - Also tax-free for medical expenses
  - BUT: use it or lose it every year (doesn't carry over)
  - You can't take it with you if you leave the company
  - Also set by IRS: max $3,400/year (2026)

Think of FSA like a yearly medical voucher.
If you don't use it by Dec 31, it disappears.

HSA vs FSA — key difference:
  HSA = like a savings account (yours forever)
  FSA = like a use-it-or-lose-it yearly wallet


---

IRS — Internal Revenue Service
--------------------------------
IRS = India's "Income Tax Department" equivalent in the USA.

They set the rules for how much you can save in HSA/FSA
without paying tax. They update these limits every year.

Our app uses IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-19 — which is the official
government document with 2026 limits.

Why does this matter for our app?
  If an employee tries to contribute MORE than IRS allows
  into their HSA, they get taxed + penalized. Our app
  shows them exactly how much room they have left.


---

HDHP — High Deductible Health Plan
-------------------------------------
Some people choose plans with HIGH deductibles (like $1,700+)
because the monthly premium is LOWER.

Trade-off:
  LOW premium (you pay less monthly)
  BUT HIGH deductible (you pay more before insurance helps)

Only people on HDHP are ALLOWED to have an HSA.
That's an IRS rule — you must have a "qualifying" high-deductible
plan to get the HSA tax benefit.


---

PPO — Preferred Provider Organization
---------------------------------------
A different type of health plan.

  - You can see ANY doctor (in-network or out-of-network)
  - Higher monthly premium
  - Lower deductible usually
  - More flexible, but more expensive month-to-month

In our app, Satyam Sangal and Arpit Singh have "Optum Family PPO"
= they chose the flexible plan that covers the whole family.
